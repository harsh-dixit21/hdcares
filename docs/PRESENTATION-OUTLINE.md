# HD Cares — presentation outline

Suggested format: 10 slides, about 6–7 minutes. This is slide-ready content and speaker guidance, not a generated PPTX.

## 1. The idea

**HD Cares — Privacy, protection, and a path to help**

- Support for threatened or actual intimate-image abuse.
- Private fingerprinting, guided reporting, and case tracking.
- Human support in one place.

Say: “Our aim is to reduce both the spread of harmful content and the confusion faced by someone trying to get help.”

## 2. The problem

- A threat or leak creates urgency and uncertainty.
- Reporting routes are scattered across services.
- People need clear next steps and trustworthy progress updates.

Use a fictional scenario: “Someone threatens to publish Asha's private picture. She needs help without sending it to another person.” Do not use real intimate imagery or unsupported statistics.

## 3. Who we help

- Adults worried about a leak.
- People dealing with already-published content.
- People seeking help with sexual blackmail or fabricated imagery.

Say: “We design especially for the barriers women face while supporting people of all genders. Content created when someone was under 18 follows a specialist referral path.”

## 4. Prevention workflow

**Choose locally → Create fingerprint → Consent → Supported protection route → Track available outcomes**

- Original image stays on the device.
- The identifier is a digital fingerprint, not an IP address.
- Protection depends on participating services.

Say: “Saving a fingerprint in our app alone does not protect it elsewhere. Our first release guides users to established services; direct integrations require partnerships.”

## 5. Already leaked workflow

**Add links → Prepare requests → User submits → Track responses → Follow up**

- Separate report for each location.
- Optional official complaint preparation.
- Separate search-result removal guidance.

Say: “We organise the process. The platform handles removal, and authorities handle complaints. We do not guarantee their decisions.”

## 6. What makes it helpful

- One case timeline with clear next steps.
- Regional-language guidance and support referrals.
- Private access and optional discreet notifications.
- User-controlled sharing and withdrawal.

Say: “Our proposed contribution is joining these steps into an understandable local support experience. We will test whether that solves a real gap.”

## 7. How the technology works

**Browser fingerprinting → Minimal-data case service → Authorised partner / reporting route**

- Exact hashes match identical files.
- Perceptual hashes can match some modified copies.
- Platforms review matches and apply their own policies.

Say: “A fingerprint is a comparison signal. It cannot tell us who leaked a photo, prove consent, or reach every website.”

## 8. Advantages and constraints

| Advantages | Constraints |
|---|---|
| Originals stay off our server | Platform participation is required |
| Less reporting confusion | Edited copies can evade matching |
| Clear progress and support | False matches need review |
| Potential re-upload prevention | No universal deletion guarantee |

Say: “We measure useful outcomes within defined coverage instead of promising an internet-wide ban.”

## 9. MVP and future scope

**First:** guided journeys, harmless-image fingerprint demo, case dashboard, reporting drafts, verified referrals.

**Next:** NGO pilot, language support, approved platform connections.

**Later:** evaluated video matching and consent-based caseworker collaboration.

Say: “The demonstration uses a mock platform. All simulated events are labelled. Real platform access is a separate milestone.”

## 10. Impact and ask

- Faster access to the right reporting route.
- Better visibility into unresolved cases.
- Less repeated effort for the affected person.

**Ask:** mentorship, safeguarding/NGO partners, and support for a limited pilot.

Close: “HD Cares helps people move from uncertainty to informed action, without asking them to upload their private image to us.”

## 45-second pitch

“When a private image is threatened or shared without consent, the person affected has to navigate multiple reporting systems while under stress. HD Cares brings prevention guidance, reporting, case tracking, and support into one place. Where fingerprinting is used, the original image stays on the user's device. For existing leaks, we help prepare requests and track each reported location. We build on established protection services rather than claiming to control the internet. Our first milestone is a privacy-tested prototype and an NGO-supported pilot, followed by authorised integrations.”

## Likely judges' questions

**Does this already exist?**
Fingerprint protection exists through services such as StopNCII and Take It Down. Our proposed value is a local, multilingual coordination and support experience. That value needs user validation.

**Can you delete an image from every website?**
No. Removal depends on the host platform. Our dashboard states coverage and tracks requests separately.

**Why would platforms connect to a student project?**
They may not initially. The first version provides guided referrals and reporting assistance. Partnerships are a later dependency, not an assumed asset.

**What if the picture is cropped or edited?**
Perceptual matching can recognise some changes but can also miss them. We will test transformations and report limitations rather than claim perfect detection.

**What stops someone from reporting an innocent person's content?**
Consent declarations, restricted access, abuse controls, platform policy review, and appeal paths. Hashes alone cannot establish ownership or consent.

**Will the complaint be filed automatically?**
The MVP prepares a draft and guides the user to the official process. Future submission automation requires an authorised interface and explicit user approval.

**Is a fingerprint anonymous?**
It contains no original image pixels, but can be matched against a known candidate. We treat it as sensitive, restrict access, and minimise associated identity data.

**How will you demonstrate the idea?**
Use a normal landscape image and a mock partner platform: show local fingerprinting, an identical-copy match, an unrelated non-match, a review event, and a status update. Show that a mock event is not a real-world takedown.
